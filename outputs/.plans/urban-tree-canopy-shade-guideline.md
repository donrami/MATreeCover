# Plan: Urban Tree Canopy "30%" Neighborhood Shade Guideline & Heat Stress

Slug: `urban-tree-canopy-shade-guideline`
Date: 2026-08-06

## 1. Objective

Literature review on the **"30% urban tree canopy" neighborhood guideline** as a
heat-stress mitigation target: where the number comes from, what quantitative
evidence supports (or fails to support) it, how heat stress is measured in that
literature, and the main critiques/open questions.

## 2. Key questions

1. **Origin:** Where does the 30% neighborhood canopy figure come from?
   (3-30-300 rule [Konijnendijk]; American Forests / Tree Equity; city canopy
   goals; older guidance.)
2. **Evidence (physical):** What is the quantitative dose–response between
   canopy cover % and heat-stress-relevant metrics (land surface temperature,
   air temperature, PET/UTCi, WBGT, shade fraction)?
3. **Evidence (health):** What is the link between neighborhood canopy % and
   heat-related mortality/morbidity (e.g., Iungman et al. 2023 European
   modeling; US city studies)?
4. **Threshold vs. round number:** Is 30% an evidence-based threshold, or a
   convenient target? Any dose-response plateaus / non-linearities?
5. **Equity:** How is 30% used as an equity target (canopy disparity by
   income/race, heat exposure disparity)?
6. **Critiques/gaps:** Feasibility, water tradeoffs, species/irrigation
   effects, maintenance, measurement methodology (LST vs air temp vs
   pedestrian-level), spatial scale issues.

## 3. Sources to consult

- **Academic (alpha / arXiv / web):** tree canopy cover + heat, 3-30-300 rule,
  urban shade thermal comfort (PET/UTCi), heat-related mortality + greenspace,
  tree equity, cooling meta-analyses (Bowler 2010; Ziter; Rahman).
- **Policy/web:** American Forests (Tree Equity Score, canopy goal documents),
  city sustainability/climate plans (Austin, Denver, Philadelphia, Vancouver,
  Melbourne, Seattle, Minneapolis), EPA heat island guidance, WHO urban health,
  press coverage of 3-30-300 adoption.
- **Time period:** 2000–2026, emphasis on 2015–2026.
