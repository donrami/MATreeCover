Primary sources re-inspected directly: Chen et al. 2022 (Nat Commun), Huang et al. 2025 (Sci Rep), Ibebuchi & Abu 2026 (*World* 7(1):6), Garrett et al. 2025 (IJHG 24:37), O'Brien et al. 2020 (AJPH), Reid et al. 2009 (EHP), Wolf & McGregor 2013, Song et al. 2024 (Environ Int), Lancet Planetary Health 9(11) buffer guidance, Zhang & Tan 2019.

## Summary

This is a genuinely useful and mostly accurate literature review. Its core framing — AWD (area-weighted canopy share of a district) measures *provision*, BWB (building/population-weighted mean canopy in a buffer) measures *exposure*, and presenting one as the other is an ecological-fallacy-class error — is correct, well-documented, and directly answers all three of the user's questions: (a) HVI and UHI practice at district level is dominated by area-weighted zonal means over census/administrative units (Niu et al. 2021, Reid et al. 2009, Wolf & McGregor 2013, NYC/DC/NSW HVIs, US SUHI DB); (b) the misinterpretation hazards (park/forest inflation, within-tract heterogeneity, metric non-interchangeability, sign/significance reversals) are documented with concrete numbers; (c) the AWD/BWB distinction is formalized in §2.2 with measured magnitudes in §5.2. The quantitative anchor points I re-checked against primary sources hold up: Garrett 8–10% built-up exposure, Kendall τ 0.56–0.89, Baltimore 47–51% → 12–14% deciles, Chen Gini 0.47/0.24 and 21.93%, Huang ≈0.5 K/decade per 10,000 persons/km²/decade, O'Brien 38% within-tract LST variance, Song 2024 RR ratios, Song 2023 RRs, and the Lancet Planetary Health buffer guidance (which the verification pass could not read — I confirmed it exists and that its recommendations match the draft's attribution).

However, the review fails at its own advertised discipline in several places: the headline summary itself commits a conflation error on the Chen et al. Gini figures; a load-bearing interpretive claim ("where greenery sits matters more than how much there is") misreads the very variance decomposition cited next to it; a directional-bias claim about densification overstates Huang et al.; the formal BWB definition has a normalization bug and a wrong asymptotic claim; and three parenthetical citations with no source entries at all are still sitting in the deliverable text. All are fixable, but the document should not ship in its current state.

## Strengths

- **S1.** The central distinction (provision vs exposure; polygon-support vs buffer-support) is stated early, kept consistent through §2.2, §5.1, and §6.5, and the "what practitioners should report" section is actionable. The three user questions are answered explicitly.
- **S2.** The quantitative comparisons are the strongest part of the draft and verified against primary texts in this review: Garrett et al. 2025 abstract states "exposure to built-up areas was higher by 8–10%" and "lower exposure to non-built-up areas compared to averaging across entire LSOAs"; the Kendall τ range 0.56–0.89 is real; O'Brien et al. 2020 abstract confirms "38%" between-street-within-tract variance and "no independent effect of the average land surface temperature of the census tract"; Song et al. 2024 confirms population-weighted exposure at 200–4000 m buffers, optimal ≈1 km, RR ratios 1.424/1.191/1.227; Reid et al. 2009 confirms 10 factors, 39,794 tracts, vegetation from satellite; Wolf & McGregor 2013 confirms 9 proxies, 4,765 census districts.
- **S3.** The hedging in §7 ("Direction of bias between AWD and BWB is configuration-dependent, not fixed… 'always overstates' is an overstatement") is exactly the right kind of epistemic honesty, and the draft's practice of marking unverifiable items inline rather than silently dropping them is good process.
- **S4.** The use of O'Brien et al. 2020 as direct evidence that tract-level area means mask within-tract variation is well chosen and correctly quoted.
- **S5.** The Gini correction (0.27 → 0.24) and the density-coefficient correction (per-1000 → per-10,000) from the verification pass are fully propagated in the body; I found no residual "0.27", "per 1000", "13:6461", or "±0.04" outside the verification header.

## Weaknesses

- [W1] **FATAL — Summary finding 4 misstates Chen et al. 2022 by conflating two different comparisons.** The sentence claims "greenspace *supply* vs *population-weighted exposure* differ in Gini inequality (0.47 for Global South vs 0.24 for Global North cities)". In Chen et al. 2022, the Gini 0.47 / 0.24 is the mean *within-city greenspace exposure* inequality for Global South vs Global North cities ("Global South cities also experience much higher inequalities in greenspace exposure (Gini: 0.47)… almost twice that of Global North cities (Gini: 0.24)"). It is not a supply-vs-exposure contrast, and the paper computes no Gini for supply at all; the supply-vs-exposure gap is a different quantity (e.g., NYC coverage 58.01% vs exposure 25.73%). The draft's headline bullet therefore commits the very metric-conflation the review exists to warn against. Section 5.2's version of the same finding is correct — the summary must be rewritten to match it.
- [W2] **MAJOR — "where greenery sits relative to people matters more than how much there is" contradicts the decomposition cited in the same sentence.** Section 5.2 (Chen et al. bullet) cites "21.93% of variance" (unique contribution of greenspace *coverage rate*, i.e., provision) and "53.42% jointly with provision × configuration", then glosses "i.e., where greenery sits relative to people matters more than how much there is". The paper's unique contributions are provision 21.93% vs configuration (edge density) 3.68% — i.e., the *unique* effect of configuration is ~6× smaller than that of provision. The "matters more" claim is contradicted by the numbers printed next to it; the 53.42% is a joint (provision × configuration) term, not configuration alone. If the point is wanted, support it with the paper's coverage-vs-exposure gap examples (NYC/Billings) and say "provision and configuration jointly dominate; the exposure metric is population-weighted, so placement relative to people is inseparable from quantity."
- [W3] **MAJOR — "area averages overstate heat-exposure trends in sprawling cities and understate them under densification" overstates Huang et al. 2025.** The paper reports 74.7% of declining-density cities showed slower population-weighted vs area-average SUHI trends, but only 56.8% of densifying cities did, and the regression intercept is negative (−0.17 K), i.e., on average population-weighted exposure rose *more slowly* even with mixed density trends. "Understate them under densification" is therefore not a supported general directional claim — it holds for only ~43% of densifying cities and the sign is near a coin flip. Soften to: "the gap widens with density decline; in densifying cities the difference is inconsistent."
- [W4] **MAJOR — the formal BWB definition has a normalization bug, a wrong asymptote, and conflates population-weighting with buffer support.** (i) `BWCanopy(D) = Σ_b w_b · c(b) with weights w_b ∝ footprint area` defines a *sum*, not the "weighted mean" the text calls it; it needs `Σ_b w_b·c(b)/Σ_b w_b` or "with normalized weights". (ii) "As r → large, BWB converges toward a population-weighted AWD" is wrong as written: once r exceeds the district's diameter every building's buffer contains the whole district, so c(b) = AWD for all b and BWB → AWD *regardless of weights*. The correct statement is that the weights become irrelevant at large r. (iii) The taxonomy implies population-weighting ⇒ buffers, but Baltimore (Ibebuchi & Abu 2026) is a population-weighted average of *CBG polygon* means (MRLC canopy zonal-mean per CBG, then population-weighted decile means and Gini) — a "population-weighted AWD", not a buffer metric. The review should state explicitly that weighting basis (area/population/buildings) and spatial support (polygon/buffer) are two orthogonal axes, and place Baltimore in the polygon-support column. This matters because the draft uses Baltimore as a flagship "population-weighted exposure" example in the Summary and §5.2.
- [W5] **MAJOR — three parenthetical citations have no source entries at all and must be resolved before delivery.** §6.2: "the 'ICUC12 microclimate study' and the 'Boston CEE 2025, 10.1038/s43247-025-02462-3' citations… have no verifiable entries in the research files"; §6.4: "the '2SFCA cooling-equity studies' cited here have no verifiable entries". The DOI 10.1038/s43247-025-02462-3 cannot be resolved from the draft's Sources list at all — a reader cannot check it. Delete these parentheticals or add real entries with URLs. The same must-fix applies to the flagged-but-unverified body claims that carry numbers: NYC HVI "median neighborhood green space ≈ 25%" (delete or verify), and Deng et al. 2024 "human-related factors more scale-sensitive than natural factors" (the directional claim itself is unverified, not just the "87 cities, 12 scales" figures — the paper was never read; verify the direction before keeping it). Good news: the Laatikainen and Graham "~15× vs >70%" items were already properly rephrased.
- [W6] **MAJOR — "Tate 2012-type uncertainty literature" is a dangling citation.** §7 Consensus: "HVI predictive validity is weak and sensitive to unit choice and indicator selection (Niu et al. 2021; Tate 2012-type uncertainty literature)." Tate (2012) has no entry in Sources. Either add the reference or drop the parenthetical.
- [W7] **MAJOR — Baltimore decile figure should be 12–14%, not 13–15%.** The paper's Results (§3.2) and Conclusions both report "≈12–14%" for the most-deprived decile; only the Discussion says "13–15%". The draft (Summary finding 4 and §5.2) uses 13–15%, and the verification report claims "13–15% confirmed" — sloppy, since the paper is internally inconsistent and the Results figure is the canonical one. Cite 12–14% (and optionally note the paper's internal discrepancy).
- [W8] **MINOR — acronyms and terms used before definition.** "MAUP" appears in Summary finding 6 and §4 but "Modifiable Areal Unit Problem" is never spelled out anywhere in the cited draft. "Gini" is used in Summary finding 4 without any definition. "3-30-300" is referenced in Summary finding 3 with no explanation of the rule (relevant for a reader who is not the parent project). Add one-line definitions at first use.
- [W9] **MINOR — the Chen et al. quote is spliced.** "measures of provision (in total or per capita) are often simplistically equated to actual exposure… aggregated unit measures of greenspace total supply or per capita supply produce an ecological fallacy—biased inferences of individual patterns from aggregated data" — the ellipsis silently drops the qualifier "on the assumption that the population is evenly exposed to greenspace over time and space" and several intervening sentences including the "Therefore," connector. Both fragments are verbatim, but presented as one continuous quote. Restore the qualifier or mark the splice explicitly (e.g., "[…]" plus a note).
- [W10] **MINOR — zombie fragment "US/global".** §5.2: "**US/global SUHI trends (Huang et al. 2025…)**" — Huang et al. covers 1,000 global cities; the "US/" prefix is a leftover from the pre-verification draft ("US SUHI trends"). Change to "Global SUHI trends".
- [W11] **MINOR — Wolf & McGregor "(SOAs)" gloss is unverified.** §3: "9 census-based proxies for 4,765 census districts (SOAs)". The paper's own abstract says "4765 census districts" (2001 census geography); nothing I could check supports the "(SOAs)" gloss (2004+ Super Output Areas are a different geography). Drop the parenthetical or verify the unit.
- [W12] **MINOR — two-study "Consensus" on canopy vs vegetation.** §7: "Canopy (trees) is a stronger heat-health-relevant green metric than total vegetation or grass at most scales (Zhang & Tan 2019; Reid et al. 2017)." Neither study is heat-specific (mental-health/wellbeing outcomes, Singapore and NYC), so the "heat-health-relevant" framing outruns the cited evidence, and two single-city health studies don't make a consensus. Soften to "some studies find" or add heat-specific support (e.g., Ziter et al. 2019 on canopy-dominant daytime cooling).
- [W13] **MINOR — park-inflation directional claim rests mostly on one study.** Summary finding 5: "district-level % canopy is inflated by parks and forest patches whose cooling does not reach residents (park cool-island air-temperature effects are small for most parks; Grace…2025)". The causal inference ("cooling does not reach residents") is presented as general but is anchored on Grace et al. 2025. Hedge explicitly ("per Grace et al., park cool-island air-temperature effects are small for most parks") or add corroboration (e.g., the park-cool-island meta-analysis literature).
- [W14] **MINOR — §5.2 Baltimore bullet juxtaposes two non-comparable statistics.** "population-weighted canopy fell from 47–51%…; area-level canopy–deprivation correlation ρ ≈ −0.51" — the decile means are population-weighted, the correlation is an unweighted CBG-level Spearman; these are different quantities, not the same measure computed two ways. A sentence making this explicit (and noting the underlying canopy is an area-zonal CBG mean, see W4) would prevent misreading.
- [W15] **MINOR — no Mermaid/taxonomy diagram.** The sibling deliverable `outputs/tree-canopy-30-heat.md` opens its taxonomy with a Mermaid flowchart. If the project convention for literature-review deliverables includes such a diagram, add a small Mermaid flowchart in §2 mapping the metric families (polygon-support vs buffer-support × weighting basis) to AWD / population-weighted AWD / BWB / population-weighted buffer exposure. Optional if the convention does not apply to this deliverable.

**Notes that passed checking (no action needed):** the Lancet Planetary Health 9(11) guidance — flagged OK-M by the verifier because the PDF was unreadable — is real (confirmed via UU/LMU repository and the Lancet issue page) and its verified recommendations (hypothesis-driven buffer choice, test multiple sizes, report rationale) match the draft's attributions; Zhang & Tan 2019 is correctly re-attributed and the "strongest associations at ~400–1600 m" claim matches; Huang et al. 2025's coefficient reading ("0.5 K/decade slower than the area-average trend per 10,000 persons/km²/decade density decline") matches the paper's own statement of the regression slope (5.05–5.29×10⁻⁵); the ecological-fallacy framing of §6.1 (Wakefield, Morgenstern) is orthodox.

## Verdict
